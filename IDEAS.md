## From the C playtest (2026-10-20)
- C's open design problem (Brian): with no mana cost, cards lose a key power differentiator; a roguelike needs tradeoffs. Directions to explore: a per-card "weight" that eats the 5-card limit or the timer (heavy cards count as 2, or shorten the next pick window), cards that only pay in a certain combo slot (opener / middle / closer), spirit-number tradeoffs (strong cards with awkward numbers), self-costs (HP, discard).
- **Brian's direction (2026-10-20): depth from keywords and synergies, like The Bazaar.** Build-defining keywords (Burn, Haste...) so cards combo with each other and a pick has context in the deck you're building, beyond a general tier list. This is the preferred route over the cost / weight ideas above. How it could meet C's combo: a keyword that scales with chain position or spirit runs (Burn stacks grow on straights), one that bends the combo rules (Haste: the next pick window is longer, or the chain limit +1), and payoff cards that read the combo so far ("+3 per Burn card earlier in this combo").
- **Brian (2026-10-20), building on that:** mechanics that favor different spirit hands: straights vs pairs, maybe other poker hands (three of a kind, full house, flush by family?). Haste extending combos is a keeper. A few build identities like this give depth, and cross-identity combo decks give the payoff: Haste + Burn gets a huge return on a long straight that a pure Burn deck can't reach. "That's the type of natural synergy that screams a good design base."
- Brian (2026-10-20): in C, block may need rethinking as a player concept: you can't aim block or damage at precise amounts at this pace, so block cards sometimes do nothing (their spirit number still has value).

## Brian's notes (2026-10-21), for C's direction
- **Relics, Slay the Spire style, written against C's keywords and moments:** "On draw, do X", "Your Burns do Y". (Relics were cut from this build's scope; this is a direction for a game made on C.) Fits the engine: draw-on-play, Burn ticks, pairs / straights and combo ends are already events a relic could listen to.
- **Finishers with recipes:** completing a certain combo or condition (e.g. a straight of 5, two pairs, 10 Burn on one skeleton) grants you that Finisher card on the spot; Finisher cards can also be drafted. Turns finishers from "a card you hope to draw" into a goal you build toward, and gives each build identity its own payoff (a Burn recipe, a Haste recipe...).
- **Brian (2026-10-21): multiple characters for C.** A defensive character in the squad with its own combos, so defensive plays feel good on their own instead of muddying or weakening the offensive plan. (More heroes were cut from this build; a direction for a game made on C.) Ties to the open question: do slower / defensive builds fit C's loop?
